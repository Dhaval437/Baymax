BAYMAX 180 mm — ASSEMBLY AND HARDWARE
1 October 2026

PRINT PROJECT
For direct printing open Baymax_180mm_P1S_PRINT.gcode.3mf in Bambu Studio.
It contains both plates already sliced. Select plate 1 or 2 and print each once.
For editing instead, open Baymax_180mm_P1S_TWO_PLATES.3mf as a project.
Estimates: plate 1 = 3 h 18 min / 104 g; plate 2 = 7 h 12 min / 149 g.
Plate 1: base and small fittings, 17 pieces.
Plate 2: torso, arms, feet and head, 10 pieces.
Print one copy of each plate. All parts use filament slot 1, ordinary PLA.
P1S / 0.4 mm nozzle / textured PEI / 0.20 mm layers / 3 walls /
15% infill / 5 top and bottom layers / tree supports / 4 mm outer brims.
Keep the supplied orientations and 100% scale. The assembled height is
180 mm including the base. Paint the raised eyes and connecting line black.
The three small rectangular pieces are optional 0.6, 1 and 2 mm servo shims.

This is a first physical prototype. Digital checks do not establish the fit
of every SG90 clone or the delivered audio boards. Remove support and burrs,
dry-fit before bonding, and do not force a tight plastic joint.

FASTENERS AND ASSEMBLY CONSUMABLES
3 x M3 x 20 mm pan/button-head screws: torso upper pair and lower left.
1 x M3 x 16 mm pan/button-head screw: torso lower right.
4 x M2.5 x 20 mm pan/button-head screws: two per arm rear cover.
6 x M2.5 x 10 mm pan/button-head screws: two per arm horn clamp,
    plus two for the head carrier/clamp sandwich.
2 x M3 x 8 mm pan/button-head screws: feet to base support beams.
4 x M3 x 12 mm COUNTERSUNK screws and 4 M3 hex nuts: base bottom cover.
2 x M2 x 5 mm COUNTERSUNK screws and 2 M2 hex nuts: talk-button bracket.
3 x stock servo horn retaining screws: supplied with the three servos.
About 16 narrow nylon cable ties, nominal 2.5 mm wide.
Small amount of PLA-compatible cyanoacrylate gel or suitable epoxy.
Black acrylic paint/fine marker for the face. Fine file/sandpaper and cutters.
Do not substitute longer screws where a motor, board or outer skin lies ahead.
Small pilot holes in the printed bosses accept screws directly, carefully
forming threads in PLA; turn by hand and stop when seated. Do not power-drive.

MECHANICAL ASSEMBLY ORDER
1. Let plates cool. Peel away brims and tree supports gradually. Support the
   tiny fingers, head locating pins and thin seam edges while cleaning.
   Clear screw passages and wire passages without enlarging bearing seats.

2. Lay out mirrored arm fronts/rears and legs using their names in the 3MF.
   Left/right names follow the CAD coordinates, not the viewer's perspective.
   Match the parts by shape. Keep their pairs together.

3. With the base bottom off, seat each leg on the shaped top openings.
   The underside beams provide the screw seats. Drive one M3 x 8 screw
   upward through each beam into the pilot in its corresponding foot.
   Route the robot harness through the larger adjacent foot/wire passages.

4. Dry-fit the torso front over both upper leg sockets with the speaker
   temporarily placed to confirm access. Bond each leg ONLY to the torso
   front socket after alignment. Keep adhesive away from the removable rear
   torso and the speaker seat. Let the adhesive cure before loading the joints.

5. Before attaching any horns, electrically command all three unloaded
   positional servos to their centre position using the test firmware.
   Power must be disconnected again before mechanical assembly. Do not turn
   a servo shaft hard by hand. Use the original plastic horns and splines;
   no printed spline is required.

6. Seat the two arm servos in the horizontal internal cradles with shafts
   pointing outward through the shoulder openings. Secure each case using
   narrow cable ties through the provided slots, avoiding the shaft and wires.
   A supplied shim may be used only if needed for the actual servo case.
   Place a short single stock horn in each arm hub relief, pointing toward
   the hand. The printed clamp holds the horn arm with two M2.5 x 10 screws.
   If the horn tip is too long, shorten only the unused tip; do not cut its
   circular hub or alter the servo spline. Fit the arm with its hand resting
   down, and install the stock retaining screw through the outer access hole.
   Fit each rear arm cover using its two M2.5 x 20 screws. Keep the round
   bearing clearance and the moving seam free of adhesive.

7. Seat the neck servo upright in its cradle, shaft toward the head and
   output axis slightly forward of the case centre. Secure it with ties.
   Sandwich its short single horn between 07_head_carrier and
   07_head_horn_clamp. The horn tip points toward the rear of the head.
   Fasten this stack into the head-front columns using two M2.5 x 10 screws
   from below. Trim only an unused horn tip if needed to fit the round pocket.
   Set the head straight at the servo's electrical centre. Insert the stock
   horn retaining screw through the roof service hole. Dry-fit the rear head
   with its two locating sockets, then bond the head seam. The completed head
   remains removable from the servo through the roof service hole.

8. Place one 52 mm speaker in the rear torso seat facing its sound toward
   the rear grille. Use the strap ears and narrow ties to secure its metal
   basket/magnet without touching or pressing the cone. Keep terminals clear.
   Fix the microphone module to the inner front pad with its acoustic port
   aligned to the small front sound hole, without covering the microphone port.

9. Secure the ESP32 on the long rails of the base bottom, the compact power
   distribution board on the rear rails, and amplifier on the small side rails.
   Use insulating supports/ties; avoid exposed conductive ends against boards.
   Leave room for headers and gentle wire bends. This design uses a small
   perfboard, not the large breadboard or MB102 regulator module.

10. Fit the talk plunger through the front base hole. Slide the retaining
    ring onto the stem inside; a tiny adhesive dot fixes ring to stem ONLY.
    It must still slide freely in the base. Clip a nominal 6 x 6 mm tactile
    switch into its bracket. Use two M2 x 5 countersunk screws from below
    and two nuts to attach the bracket. Slide its slots until pressing the
    plunger clicks the switch and release returns it freely, then tighten.
    Actual switch actuator height may require bracket adjustment.

11. Feed the low-voltage power cable through the removable rear insert's
    8 mm hole before connecting its inner end. Secure strain relief using
    the provided tie slots. This is a cable entry, not a universal barrel-jack
    mounting hole. Any matching adapter socket/pigtail sits insulated inside.
    The base bottom captures the insert. Do not rely on glue alone for strain
    relief. Do not route mains voltage into the printed robot.

12. Keep the rear torso off while the hardware is first tested. The digital
    motion check covers a 30-degree forward arm lift and head yaw +/-20 degrees
    in 2-degree steps. Begin physical tests at a much smaller range and low
    speed, one joint at a time. Servo direction and zero offset depend on
    installation; never start with a full 0-to-180-degree sweep.

13. After wiring and motion tests, close the rear torso using the four M3
    screws listed above, then fit the base bottom with its four M3 nuts and
    countersunk screws. Fit the two arm access plugs and the roof plug with
    light removable retention (tiny reusable adhesive dots if loose).
    Bond the chest badge in its recess. Keep the service plugs removable.

POWER AND BENCH TOOLS
Reuse the ESP32-S3 N16R8, one speaker, one existing SG90 and jumper wires.
Buy two matching positional SG90 servos, an enclosed regulated 5 V / 5 A
adapter, a matching >=5 A low-voltage input pigtail, compact 50 x 30 mm
perfboard, and 1000 uF / >=10 V capacitor. INMP441 and MAX98357A are ordered.
Reuse suitable power wire if owned; thin signal Dupont wire is not a main
motor supply harness. Use short insulated 20 AWG copper supply runs.
Borrow soldering equipment or have a technician make and insulate the harness.
A basic digital multimeter is needed for polarity and continuity checks.
Servos and amplifier receive separate branches from the 5 V distribution,
with common ground. Do not power motors through the ESP32 3.3 V regulator
or the MB102. Do not connect external 5 V and laptop USB simultaneously
until the delivered board's power-path isolation has been checked.
The printed package does not replace the later electrical commissioning.
